Rules & Hardware

Rules & The Starter Kit

Every team starts with the exact same electronics package and fair competition rules—winning comes down to smart engineering, clean wiring, and driver control.

Competition Rules

Weight Class & Victory Conditions

All robots compete in the Ultra-Lightweight class under 150 grams. There are two paths to victory in the arena:

  • Knockout: Immobilize or damage the opposing robot.
  • Ring-Out: Push your opponent off the arena platform.
Provided to Every Team

Standardized Hardware Kit

  • 2x Drive Motors (N20) for agile two-wheel locomotion
  • ESP32 Microcontroller — the wireless "brain" of the bot
  • BLHeli 20A ESC & Brushless Motor for active weapon power
  • LiPo Battery & Master Switch for safe power delivery
  • 3D-Print Filament & Base Platform for custom armor
Inside the Kit

From Wiring Diagrams to Soldered Harnesses

Before printing a single armor plate, students learn how to wire a safe power circuit: XT60 battery lead, master switch, BLHeli speed controller, and motor leads.

CAD & Engineering

Student Robot Concepts

From horizontal tri-blade spinners to dual-servo flippers and clamp bots: every group designs a custom combat strategy in Autodesk Fusion (click any image to inspect).

Archetype 01 · Horizontal Spinner

Tri-Blade Spinner & Angled Hull

Wide front strike radius for high kinetic energy impacts, paired with a low-profile chamfered chassis that shields the battery and ESP32.

Archetype 02 · Flipper & Wedge

Dual-Servo Flipper

Slides underneath rival bots with a sharp ground-hugging front wedge and launches them with twin synchronized high-torque servos.

Archetype 03 · Drum Spinner

Drum Spinner with Wedgelets

Compact high-speed rotating drum nested between four front forks that feed opponents directly into upward-striking teeth.

Archetype 04 · Control & Grabber

Dual-Arm Clamp Bot

Captures opponents between its front wedge and articulated overhead claws, giving drivers complete control to push rivals off the stage.

From CAD to Bench

90% Custom 3D-Printed Chassis

Once a team's CAD assembly passes interference checks, hulls, top plates, and weapon hubs are sliced and printed in Hyper-PLA, PETG, or tough TPU—tuned down to the gram so every robot weighs in under 150g.

Program Roadmap

6-Week Build Timeline

A structured engineering sprint taking student teams from whiteboard sketches to competition-ready drivers.

Phase 01 · Kickoff

Concept & Weapon Choice

  • Team formation (2–3 students)
  • Brainstorming & paper blueprints
  • Weapon choice (Spinner, Flipper, Wedge)
Phase 02 · Week 1

Base & Electronics

  • Assemble the standard base
  • Solder & wire motors and ESP32
  • Initial drive & radio tests
Phase 03 · Weeks 2–4

CAD & Weapon Build

  • Custom weapon design in CAD
  • 3D printing & mechanical assembly
  • ESC & safety switch integration
Phase 04 · Weeks 5–6

Tuning & Drive Practice

  • Strict weight check (< 150g)
  • Driver control & steering practice
  • Match strategy & durability fixes
